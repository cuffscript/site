title: Why CuffScript
---

The first wall most people hit when they start learning to program is not logic but **unfamiliar symbols and strict rules**. After a missing semicolon produces a cryptic error message, it is easy to conclude "I can't think logically", when the real problem is only that you haven't memorized the language's rules yet.

CuffScript started from the idea of lowering that first wall. It uses keywords close to the words people actually use (`set`, `change`, `do:`, `end`), and when something is wrong it tells you where and why. Each of these choices adds up to an experience where you can understand what you are doing. That doesn't cost expressiveness: functions, asynchronous execution and regex-style pattern matching are all there, and it is a real, working language for small automation scripts or game logic.

The "Cuff" in the name means a sleeve cuff. Like a tool that sits lightly on your wrist and is always within reach, the hope is that you can put the language on without effort and start making something right away. It suits people who have always been afraid of regular expressions, and people who want to run an idea immediately without setting up a project.
